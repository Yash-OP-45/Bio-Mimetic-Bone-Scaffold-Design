# Bio-Mimetic-Bone-Scaffold-Design
Conducted under the supervision of Prof. Niraj Sinha

# Objective

This project focuses on the design of a bio-inspired bone scaffold that mimics the natural graded architecture of human bone. The goal is to reduce stress shielding, improve load transfer, and enhance biological functionality through controlled porosity and optimized structural design.

# Methodology

A functionally graded TPMS-based gyroid scaffold was developed using mathematical modeling, topology optimization, CAD generation, finite element analysis (FEA), and flow analysis. Topology optimization was used to determine optimal material distribution, which was then mapped to graded gyroid geometry and validated through structural and permeability analyses.

# Results

The proposed scaffold demonstrated more uniform stress distribution, reduced stress concentrations, and improved permeability compared to conventional lattice structures. The integrated design workflow highlights the potential of TPMS-based architectures for next-generation bone tissue engineering applications.
